This privacy notice explains why the Office of the Public Guardian (OPG) collects personal data when you use the ‘Make a lasting power of attorney’ service.

This privacy notice also explains how we store this data, what we do with it, how you can get a copy of the data we have about you, and how you can complain if you think we’ve done something wrong.

You should read this notice alongside:

  • OPG’s personal information charter (opens in new tab), which sets out the standards you can expect when we ask for, use or share your personal information
  • OPG’s lasting power of attorney privacy notice (opens in new tab), which explains how we use and protect your data once we’ve received your paper LPA application
  • GOV.UK Pay’s privacy notice (opens in new tab)

Who manages the Make a lasting power of attorney service

This service is managed by OPG, which is an executive agency sponsored by the Ministry of Justice (MoJ).

MoJ is the data controller for the personal data we store. OPG’s personal information charter (opens in new tab) explains how we process personal data.

You can find more information about using the 'Make a lasting power of attorney' service in the terms of use (opens in new tab) and our cookies policy (opens in new tab).

Why we collect personal data and how we use it

In order to use this service, you need to enter personal information about the donor and the people they want to be involved in their LPA. For example: names, contact details and dates of birth.

We use the information you provide to populate your LPA application, and for no other purpose.

You can find out more about how we use the data once you have posted the LPA to us in the lasting power of attorney privacy notice.

The personal data we collect

We only collect the personal data that is relevant for the services we are providing to you.

The personal data we collect includes:

  • the name, address, email address and date of birth of the donor, attorneys and replacement attorneys
  • the name and address of the correspondent, the certificate provider and any people to notify

If the donor is applying to pay a reduced or no fee, we will also ask for:

  • evidence that the donor receives certain benefits
  • evidence of the donor’s income

This service also contains several free-text fields. You should not enter sensitive information into these free-text fields unless absolutely necessary for your application. Sensitive information may include, but is not limited to, details of religious beliefs and financial information.

Legal basis for processing this data

By registering for and using this service, you consent to us using the data you enter to help you make an LPA application that you can print, sign, and post to the OPG.

Your use of this online service is optional. If you prefer, you can make an LPA using a paper or PDF form. If you post an LPA application to us, it will be processed in line with OPG's lasting power of attorney privacy notice (opens in new tab).

Cookies and site usage data

OPG and the Government Digital Service collects site usage information from this service. This allows us to see how the service is being used so we can improve it.

This information does not contain any personal data and cannot be traced back to you.

How long we keep your data

We keep a copy of your LPA for up to 90 days from the date you last used the service. This means you can sign back in and continue your application if you do not finish it in one session.

After 90 days we automatically delete the data.

Sharing your data

We will not share your personal data with anyone else unless the law allows or requires us to.

If you pay your LPA application fee online, you will be directed to GOV.UK Pay. If you choose to pay by card, you’ll need to provide:

  • your card number
  • the expiry date
  • your card verification value (CVV)

OPG will not store these card details.

You can find out more about how GOV.UK Pay uses and protects your data in the GOV.UK Pay privacy notice (opens in new tab).

Access your personal information

You can find out what personal data we hold about you in the service by making a subject access request.

Find out more about subject access requests (opens in new tab).

Getting more information

You can get more details on:

  • agreements we have with other organisations for sharing information
  • when we can pass on personal information without telling you, for example, to help with the prevention or detection of crime or to produce anonymised statistics
  • instructions we give to staff on how to collect, use or delete your personal data
  • how we check that the information we hold is accurate and up-to-date
  • how to make a complaint
  • anything in this privacy notice
  • what to do if you think that your personal data has been misused or mishandled

Changes to this policy

We may change this privacy policy from time to time. When we do, we’ll update the ‘last updated’ date at the bottom of this page.

If these changes affect how your personal data is processed, OPG will take reasonable steps to let you know.

Any changes to this privacy policy will apply to you and your data immediately.
